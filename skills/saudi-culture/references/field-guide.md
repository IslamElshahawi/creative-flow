# Saudi cultural field guide — research snapshot

Read the provenance rules in `../SKILL.md` before using these details. Validate any `[inferred]` point that determines a campaign route with a Saudi reviewer.

## 1. Wardrobe — the vocabulary you need to specify

Currently the highest-value section here, because a route that says "a young Saudi man" and nothing else
gets whatever the model averages — which is usually a generic Gulf-adjacent costume that reads wrong.

### Men

| Garment | What it is | Specification notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Thobe / thawb** (ثوب) | The ankle-length long-sleeved robe; standard daily dress | Saudi cut has a **collar and cuffs, often with a top button placket** — visibly different from the collarless Emirati *kandura* with its tassel. Say "Saudi-cut thobe with a collar." |
| **Ghutra** (غترة) | The square head cloth, worn folded over the head | **Plain white** is the everyday and formal default, worn year-round |
| **Shemagh** (شماغ) | The red-and-white checked head cloth | Same garment role as the ghutra, different cloth. Cooler months, and a strong national-visual signal |
| **Egal / iqal** (عقال) | The doubled black cord that holds the head cloth | Present in formal and most public contexts; absent in casual/domestic |
| **Bisht** (بشت) | The sheer gold-trimmed cloak worn over the thobe | **Ceremonial only** — weddings, graduations, formal occasions, dignitaries. Putting one on a man buying groceries is a costume error |
| **Tagiyah** (طاقية) | The white skullcap under the head cloth | Usually unseen; matters if the head cloth comes off |

**Seasonal and register notes.** White thobe in warm months; darker or heavier weaves in winter.
Younger men in casual settings frequently wear **Western casual** — a route showing a 22-year-old in
jeans and a tee at a café is not inaccurate, and insisting on a thobe in every frame is its own kind of
outsider tell. `[inferred]`

### Women

| Garment | What it is | Specification notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Abaya** (عباية) | The over-garment worn in public | Historically plain black; contemporary abayas span colours, cuts, embroidery and open-front styles. **"Black abaya" is a specification; "abaya" alone is not** |
| **Tarha / shayla** (طرحة / شيلة) | The head scarf | Draping style varies widely and is a real characterisation choice |
| **Niqab** (نقاب) | Face covering | Worn by some, not all. A casting decision with meaning — make it deliberately |
| **Hijab styles** | | Coverage practice varies by family, region and setting |

**The single most common styling error is treating Saudi women's dress as one uniform.** `[inferred]`
Coverage practice varies genuinely — by region, generation, family and setting. What is consistent is
**modesty of silhouette**: shoulders, chest, midriff and legs covered.
`[verified: media.co.uk — Saudi cultural advertising guidance]` That is a floor, not a full description,
and the floor applies to men too.

### Children

Generally Western-style children's clothing day to day; traditional dress for occasions and Eid.
`[inferred]`

## 2. Casting

**Ages and groupings that read true.** `[inferred]` Multi-generational grouping is a strong and welcome
frame — grandparents, parents and children in one shot is culturally legible in a way a couple alone
often is not.

**Mixed-gender framing is a real constraint with a creative answer.** Physical contact between unrelated
men and women and romantic framing are not acceptable; **family framing is.**
`[verified: media.co.uk]` Treat this as an art-direction input rather than an obstacle: a mother and
daughter, a group of friends of the same gender, a family at a table, a woman alone in her own frame.
The constraint is often what makes the work specific.

**A very young population.** Median age is low and the youth market is dominant — but young does not mean
unserious or price-insensitive. `[inferred]`

**Saudi vs. expat casting is a decision, not a default.** Roughly a third of the population are
non-Saudi residents. A brand speaking to Saudis casts Saudis; a brand speaking to Riyadh broadly may
legitimately cast a mix. **Name the choice in the route** rather than letting the model decide — models
default hard toward Western faces unless directed otherwise, which is a named drift risk in
a current Saudi compliance review.

## 3. Home, hospitality and the objects in frame

Set dressing is where foreign-made work gives itself away fastest.

**The majlis** (مجلس) — the formal sitting room where guests are received. Floor-level or low seating
around the walls, cushions, patterned carpet, often a distinct room from where the family sits day to
day. A Saudi living room staged as a Western open-plan lounge with a sofa facing a TV is not wrong for
every household, but it is a *different room* from a majlis and shouldn't be labelled one. `[inferred]`

**Arabic coffee — qahwa** (قهوة عربية). The single most legible hospitality signal available:

- **Dallah** (دلة) — the long-spouted brass or steel pot. Instantly readable as Saudi.
- **Finjan** (فنجان) — the small handleless cup. Filled **only partway**, not to the brim.
- Qahwa is pale gold-green, not dark — cardamom-heavy, often with saffron. **A dark espresso-coloured
  liquid in a finjan is wrong**, and it is the kind of detail that has to be specified or the model will
  render coffee-as-it-knows-it.
- Served **with dates** (تمر), usually on a separate dish.
- Poured by the host, right hand, guest first.

**Dates** (تمر) — a hospitality staple in their own right, and a strong visual.

**The right hand** is used for eating, giving and receiving. `[inferred]` Worth a line in any route where
a hand offers or takes something, because it is invisible when right and jarring when wrong.

**Shoes off** indoors in many homes. `[inferred]`

## 4. Rhythm — daily, weekly, annual

Getting the *time* of an ad wrong is as visible as getting the clothes wrong.

**The day.** Life in Saudi runs late. Evening is the social centre of gravity — shops, restaurants and
family visiting run well past what a European or American ad would treat as late.
**Summer is nocturnal**, because daytime heat makes outdoor life impractical: **daytime outdoor imagery
in summer reads as false.** `[inferred]` A summer route set outdoors belongs after dark.

**Prayer punctuates the day** — five times, with commerce pausing around them. `[inferred]` Rarely
depicted directly in commercial work, but it shapes when scenes plausibly happen.

**The weekend is Friday–Saturday.** Friday is the congregational prayer day and the family day; a
"Friday night out" framing borrowed from a Western calendar lands differently.

**Ramadan inverts everything.** The nocturnal rhythm becomes total: fasting by day, *iftar* at sunset
breaking the fast, *suhoor* before dawn, family gathering, generosity and hospitality as the dominant
register. Visually: warm, lantern-lit, gathered, night-time. It is the largest commercial season of the
year and media costs peak. `[inferred, widely reported]`

**Eid al-Fitr** follows Ramadan — new clothes, gifting, colour, celebration, a short intense window.
**Eid al-Adha** — travel, family, sacrifice, gifting.

**Islamic-calendar dates move ~11 days earlier each Gregorian year.** Always verify the current year's
dates; never inherit last year's. See current Saudi market research for the current planning target.

**Fixed-date moments.** Founding Day, 22 February — heritage and history, a deeper register than
National Day. Saudi National Day, 23 September — green, flag, contemporary pride, Vision 2030 energy,
and very heavy commercial participation, so hard to stand out in.

**Entertainment seasons.** **Riyadh Season** and **Jeddah Season** are major commercial and social
windows with their own visual vocabulary and enormous attention. They are frequently missed by foreign
planners and belong in any annual calendar for the market.

## 5. Humour and tone

Least documented section here and the one most in need of native review.

**Gentle, family-friendly, culturally informed comedy works.**
`[verified: media.co.uk]` **Jokes about religion, authority figures, or sensitive cultural topics do
not** — and that is a floor, not a style guide.

`[inferred]` Beyond that floor: self-deprecation and everyday-observational humour travel well;
regional-stereotype humour is a live genre between Saudis and a bad idea for an outside brand;
sarcasm aimed *at the viewer* reads as rude in a way it may not in Western advertising.

**Indirectness.** Politeness norms favour warmth and indirect refusal. Aggressive hard-sell copy — the
"buy now or miss out" register — sits less comfortably than it does in US direct response. `[inferred]`
This is a claim worth testing rather than trusting, and it is precisely the kind of thing a Saudi
copywriter would correct in a sentence.

**National pride is an active, usable register**, not just a compliance minefield — heritage, Vision
2030, and contemporary Saudi confidence. `[verified: media.co.uk]` The caution is volume: at National
Day everyone is doing it.

## 6. What makes work read as foreign-made

The tells, in rough order of how fast a viewer clocks them:

1. **Casting drifting Western** because nothing in the prompt pinned it.
2. **Costume errors** — a bisht in a supermarket, an Emirati kandura on a Saudi man, one uniform abaya.
3. **Coffee rendered as espresso** in a finjan; a dallah with dark liquid; a full cup.
4. **Bright midday sun in summer** for an outdoor scene.
5. **Translated-sounding Arabic** — English word order in Arabic words. See `arabic-ad-copy`.
6. **A majlis that is a Western living room.**
7. **Mixed-gender staging** that ignores the framing norms.
8. **Western holiday logic** — a weekend that starts Friday evening, seasonal cues from the wrong hemisphere.
9. **Generic "Middle East"** — Moroccan lanterns, Egyptian architecture, Levantine food standing in for Saudi.

That last one is worth its own line. **The Gulf is not interchangeable, and the Arab world is much less
so.** Specify Saudi, and specify which part of Saudi.

## 7. Region

`[inferred]` — widely repeated, none of it traced to a primary source. Useful as a starting hypothesis
for casting and register; **do not present any of it to a client as fact.**

- **Riyadh** (Najd, central) — corporate, fast, status-aware, high disposable income, the government and
  business centre. More conservative in public register than Jeddah.
- **Jeddah** (Hijaz, west) — historically cosmopolitan through the Red Sea and Hajj traffic, more
  socially relaxed, more visually expressive taste.
- **Eastern Province** (Dammam, Khobar, Dhahran) — industrial, family-oriented, utility-forward, distinct
  local identity, proximity to Bahrain.
- **South** (Asir, Jazan, Najran, Al-Baha) — mountainous and green in parts, visually unlike the rest of
  the country, its own dress and architecture, culturally closer to Yemen in places.
- **Secondary cities** — more conservative in register; national and family framing travels better than
  metropolitan cool.

**Name the target region in the route.** It changes casting, wardrobe, dialect (`arabic-ad-copy`), pace
and register. "Saudi Arabia" as a single audience is the same mistake as "Europe."
