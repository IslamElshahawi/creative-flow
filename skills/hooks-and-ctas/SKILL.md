# Hooks and CTAs

Read the audience, product benefit, offer, platform, target duration and any
approved creative angle. Use current audience evidence when choosing a format.

1. Name the emotional mechanism: aspiration, self-recognition, relief from a
   specific friction, social proof, curiosity or possibility-framed urgency.
2. Choose a concrete opening format from `references/full-guide.md` when needed:
   POV, before/after, mistake call-out, storytime or another format suited to
   the audience. Test alternatives; no format always wins.
3. Sketch hook, build and payoff. Show how the stakes rise. Phrase urgency as
   a real opportunity, and verify every implied scarcity or performance claim.
4. Hand an emotional-shape brief to `arabic-ad-copy`: mechanism, format,
   intended feeling, supported product fact and CTA purpose. The copy skill
   writes the actual Arabic in one register and checks gendered forms.

Completion: a writer can draft three distinct openings from the brief without
inventing claims or guessing the intended audience response. Review the final
Arabic with a native reader. Load the guide for mechanism examples, format
options, worked rewrites and CTA patterns.
