# Hooks and CTAs — full guide

> **Written from marketing-psychology research, not from a Saudi-specific study of this exact
> mechanism.** The persuasion mechanisms below are broadly evidenced in direct-response and behavioral
> marketing literature; the *calibration* of how hard to push them is Saudi-specific and traces to
> `saudi-culture`. Treat any specific Arabic line below as a shape to adapt, not a script to lift —
> `arabic-ad-copy` still owns the actual words.

## Why this file exists

`arabic-ad-copy` already has an 8-mechanism hook bank and a CTA bank. They are correct about *grammar* —
verb-first rhythm, register, which words commit you to Najdi vs Hijazi — but they're explicit that the
bank was "constructed from sourced dialect features, not observed in running campaigns," and it treats
each mechanism as "a pattern to fill," not a claim about *why* that pattern moves anyone. This file is
the layer underneath: for each mechanism, what emotional work it's actually doing, so a script isn't just
grammatically correct Saudi Arabic wrapped around a flat fact.

## The core diagnostic: flat vs. activating

A **flat** line states a fact or issues an order. It can be entirely true and still land as nothing:

- Flat (fact): "القماش يمتص العرق." (The fabric absorbs sweat.)
- Flat (command): "اطلبيه الحين." (Order it now.) — true CTA, zero reason attached.

An **activating** line makes the fact or the order matter to a specific felt state:

- Activating: "ولا مرة حسيت إني لازم أفكر في التمرين لأن اللبس مو مريح." (Not once did I have to think
  about the workout because the outfit wasn't comfortable.) — same claim (breathable, sweat-managing
  fabric), reframed as *relief from a named friction* (having to think about your clothes mid-workout).

The test isn't "is this a fact or is this emotional" — most good ad lines carry a fact. The test is
whether the fact is doing emotional work or just sitting there waiting for the viewer to supply the
reason to care themselves. Viewers scrolling a reel don't supply that reason; the line has to.

`[verified: multiple 2026 direct-response marketing sources — gethookd.ai, digicom.io, jeremymac.com,
nflowtech.com]` Underneath every high-performing hook is a named psychological mechanism (curiosity,
loss aversion, identity, social proof, surprise) rather than volume or hype, and a hook has to do one of
two things in under ~2 seconds: open a loop the brain wants closed, or trigger an emotional response
strong enough to interrupt the scroll. `[verified: nflowtech.com, citing comparative ad-performance
research]` Emotional campaigns have been measured at roughly **28% higher conversion** than purely
rational ones — but the strongest ads combine an emotional entry point with a factual payoff, not one or
the other exclusively. That combination — emotion first, logic to justify the decision second — is the
structural reason this skill hands off to `arabic-ad-copy` rather than replacing it: the emotional shape
opens the door, the correctly-registered Arabic fact closes it.

## Persuasion-mechanism catalog

Mapped onto `arabic-ad-copy`'s existing 8 hook mechanisms so the two files describe the same lines from
two different layers — this file adds *why*, that file supplies *how to say it correctly*.

| Mechanism (this file) | What it's doing emotionally | Maps to `arabic-ad-copy` hook bank row |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspiration / identity** | "This is who I want to be" — the viewer imagines themselves already changed. | Subject-first (marked emphasis), POV |
| **Self-recognition** | "This is my exact problem, named precisely." Specificity beats generality — a vague problem doesn't feel personal. | Direct address, Question |
| **Relief from a named friction** | The specific, small annoyance (not the big abstract problem) goes away. Concrete beats abstract — "not having to think about your clothes" lands harder than "comfort." | POV / direct-experience, Bold claim |
| **Social proof** | "People like me already chose this" — belonging, not just popularity. | Social proof, Authority/stat |
| **Curiosity gap** | An open loop the brain wants closed — a question or partial reveal that withholds the payoff by one beat. | Question, Pattern interrupt |
| **Possibility-framed urgency** | What becomes *available* now, not what's *threatened* if you wait. The difference between an invitation and a warning. | (new — CTA layer, see below) |

`[verified: seozoom.com, whatconverts.com, medium.com/@support_98349 — conversion-copywriting research]`
The eight hardwired triggers most consistently cited across this literature are authority, scarcity,
social proof, reciprocation, commitment, contrast, urgency, and personalization — the mechanisms above
are this skill's Saudi-calibrated shortlist of the ones that travel well into a warmth-forward register;
scarcity and urgency in particular need the possibility-not-threat reframe below before they're usable
here at all.

## Escalation: Problem → Agitate → Relief (not Problem → Agitate → Fear)

`[verified: crazyegg.com, beomniscient.com, blaksheepcreative.com — the Problem-Agitate-Solve
copywriting framework]` PAS names a real, well-evidenced structure: state a relatable problem, intensify
the felt discomfort of it, then resolve it. The standard Western version of "agitate" leans on fear and
consequence ("keep ignoring this and it gets worse"). **Saudi calibration changes what "agitate" means,
not whether to use the structure**: intensify the *specificity* of the friction (make the viewer picture
their exact morning, their exact bag not zipping, their exact workout cut short) rather than the *threat*
of consequence. Escalate recognition, not dread.

Applied to a 3-beat reel structure (mirroring the hook/build/payoff beat shape):

1. **Hook (0–~2s)** — self-recognition or aspiration mechanism, states the specific friction or the
   specific identity, no product yet.
2. **Build (~2–~10s)** — relief-from-friction or social proof, product enters as the thing that resolves
   what beat 1 named, escalating specificity (not volume) as it goes.
3. **Payoff / CTA (final beats)** — possibility-framed urgency plus an invitation-form CTA (see below).

| الطقم مريح ومناسب للتمرين. (The set is comfortable and suitable for training.) | لأول مرة ألبس طقم رياضي ما أفكر فيه وأنا أتمرن. (For the first time I'm wearing a workout set I don't think about while I'm training.) | Self-recognition + relief |
| فافيتال يقدم جودة عالية. (Vavital offers high quality.) | بنات كثير رجعوا يطلبوا نفس الطقم بعد أول مرة. (A lot of girls came back to order the same set after the first time.) | Social proof |
| اطلبي الطقم الحين. (Order the set now, bare command.) | اكتشفي ليش هالطقم صار المفضل عند بنات كثير. (Discover why this set became so many girls' favorite.) | Possibility-framed urgency as invitation |

## CTA-specific guidance: invitation beats command

`[verified: proxdigitalagency.co.uk, oreateai.com — CTA psychology research]` Invitational phrasing
("find out how," "discover," "join") consistently outperforms commanding/loss-framed phrasing ("don't
miss this") in the literature this file drew on — people respond to a CTA that reads as their own choice,
not an instruction. This lines up directly with `saudi-culture`'s indirectness note: it isn't a
Saudi-specific finding, but the two point the same direction, which is exactly why this skill treats
"invitation over command" as close to a hard rule for this market rather than an optional style choice.

Concretely, prefer CTA verbs that already read as an invitation over ones that read as an order:

- Prefer **اكتشفي / جربي / تعرّفي على** (discover / try / get to know — feminine imperative, see the
  grammar note in `SKILL.md`) over a bare **اطلبي الحين** with no reason attached.
- If a bare order-verb CTA is what the brief needs (checkout-page copy, a bottom-of-funnel remarketing
  cut), that's `arabic-ad-copy`'s CTA bank territory, not this skill's — this skill's job is upstream of
  that, in top/mid-funnel hook-heavy creative where the viewer hasn't decided to buy yet and a command
  reads as presumptuous.
- Scarcity/urgency claims still have to be true (a current Saudi compliance review) — "لفترة محدودة" only survives if
  the offer really is time-boxed.

## Viral TikTok hook-format library, calibrated for Saudi

The mechanisms above are the *why*. This section is the *what* — named, platform-native hook formats
that have demonstrated real pull on TikTok specifically, not generic "attention-grabbing" advice. Use a
mechanism to choose which format fits, then use the format as the concrete shape for the line.

`[verified: opus.pro — analysis of 34,635 TikTok clips; influee.co, imagine.art, zeely.ai, retiplex.com —
2026 TikTok ad-hook guides]` TikTok's algorithm makes its first distribution decision in roughly 1.5
seconds, and about 65% of viewers decide whether to keep watching within the first 3 seconds — a winning
hook stays under roughly 12 spoken words. This is the same order of magnitude as `arabic-ad-copy`'s own
WARC/Lumen eye-tracking figures (~1.4–1.7s), not a contradicting number — treat the two as corroborating.
The five-part shape underneath a working TikTok ad is **hook → native-feeling delivery → one value point
→ proof → a CTA that matches the hook's register** — a script that nails the hook and then reverts to
ad-copy voice for the body has still failed.

### The format table

| Format | Opener pattern | Mechanism it serves | KSA-specific note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **POV** | "POV: أنت..." (POV: you're...) — drops the viewer into a first-person scenario | Self-recognition / identification | Travels well — the format itself is register-neutral; write the scenario in White Saudi colloquial, not MSA. |
| **Before/after reveal** | A visible state, then a hard cut to the changed state | Aspiration + relief-from-friction | `[verified: thehovi.com, marketinghouseeg.com]` **This is the single strongest-performing format specifically in Saudi TikTok data**, not just a generic trend — treat it as the default reach-for, not one option among many. |
| **"نبغى نعترف" / "تعترفون معايا" style relatability opener** (Western equivalent: "tell me without telling me," "am I the only one") | Invites the viewer to see themselves in a specific, narrow behavior | Self-recognition | `[verified]` Generates the highest comment counts of any format because it invites participation — good for a CTA that asks a question, not just "buy now." |
| **Curiosity-gap opener** ("ليش محد قالي إن..." — why did nobody tell me that...) | Withholds the payoff by one beat | Curiosity gap | Pairs naturally with `arabic-ad-copy`'s existing register-neutral question-hook row — don't duplicate the وش/إيش dialect fork inside a curiosity opener unless the brief has already picked a region. |
| **Mistake call-out** ("توقفي عن..." / "غلطة كل الوحدات يسوونها") | Names a specific, narrow error the viewer recognizes | Self-recognition, escalating to relief | **Calibration required**: the raw Western form ("Stop doing X") is a bare command. Per this skill's own possibility-framed-urgency rule, soften to an invitation shape — name the mistake, then let the relief-from-friction payoff (not a command) close it, matching `saudi-culture`'s indirectness note rather than the Western hard-sell version. |
| **Countdown / numbered list** ("٣ علامات إن...") | A bounded, scannable structure that promises a specific payoff count | Curiosity gap + structure | Safe, register-neutral, works in any dialect rung. |
| **Comparison / "what I ordered vs. what I got"** | Two states shown side by side | Social proof or relief-from-friction, depending on which side is the brand | Substantiation rule still applies (a current Saudi compliance review) — the "got" side has to be a real, traceable claim, not an invented worst-case competitor. |
| **Get Ready With Me (GRWM) / Day in the Life** | The product appears inside a real routine, not as the subject of the shot | Aspiration + relief-from-friction (fit-into-life) | `[verified: thehovi.com]` KSA audiences respond strongly to **behind-the-scenes/authenticity** framing — this format is a natural fit for that preference, more than a hard-sell demo would be. |
| **Storytime** | A short personal narrative with the product as the turn in the story | Self-recognition → relief, escalating like a micro-PAS arc | Good vehicle for the escalation rule already stated above — the format *is* the Problem→Agitate→Relief shape in miniature. |
| **Green-screen commentary / reaction** | The speaker reacts to or annotates something on screen behind them | Authority/curiosity, depending on what's reacted to | Lower priority for a locked single-brand ad — this format's strength is riding someone else's trending clip, which a brand doesn't control; more relevant to organic amplification strategy than to a scripted paid spot. |
| **Contrarian / hot-take opener** | States an unexpected or counter-consensus claim | Pattern interrupt | **Compliance gate, not just a style note**: `arabic-ad-copy` already flags this exact mechanism as needing a current Saudi compliance review before use, because a hot take can shade into "challenging Saudi social norms." Run that check before writing one, don't reach for it as a default spice. |

### Duration and pacing, KSA-specific

`[verified: thehovi.com — Saudi TikTok marketing guide, 2026]` 10–20 second videos dominate engagement in
Saudi TikTok specifically, with the visual hook expected in the first 3 seconds — front-load the hook even
harder than the general TikTok guidance above suggests, and treat a 45-second, three-beat ad structure
(this pipeline's usual shape) as needing its *first* beat to fully justify itself in that ~3-second window
before the viewer has any reason to trust the rest will pay off.

### Language register, restated for this section specifically

`[verified: thehovi.com]` Modern Standard Arabic reads as formal and disconnected on Saudi TikTok
specifically — Saudi colloquialisms and currently-trending local phrasing outperform it. This is the same
register guidance `arabic-ad-copy`'s dialect ladder already gives generally; the point of restating it
here is that a hook format borrowed from English-language TikTok research is not "translated" into White
Saudi, it's *written* in it from the first draft, per `arabic-ad-copy`'s own never-translate rule — a
literally-translated "POV:" opener followed by stiff MSA prose has the right format and the wrong
execution.

## Handoff contract

This skill's output is: one named mechanism per hook/CTA candidate, plus a neutral or bilingual gloss of
the felt experience the line should create — never finished Arabic copy. `arabic-ad-copy` takes that
gloss, picks the register, and writes the actual Arabic sentence, checking it against its own dialect
ladder, hook-bank grammar, and claim-language rules. If a gloss doesn't map cleanly onto any of
`arabic-ad-copy`'s existing hook-bank rows, that's a signal the mechanism needs a new row added there —
raise it rather than forcing a fit.
